01

Three words and a borrowed ampersand

Sourceindex.html · nav

There is no symbol. The identity is the name set in Bricolage Grotesque with one character — the ampersand — lifted into Instrument Serif italic and dropped to stone. That single substitution is the whole mark, and it is the device the rest of the brand repeats: one warm, drawn thing inside something otherwise flat and modern.

Primary — soil ground Beets & Co
Reversed — paper ground Beets & Co
On clay — near-white only Beets & Co
On beet-deep — paper only Beets & Co

Clear space & minimum size

Clear space on all four sides equals the cap height of the B. Nothing crosses it — no photo edge, no rule, no veil boundary. Minimum width 104 px / 30 mm for the three-word lockup. Below that, set the name in running type rather than shrinking the mark; there is no stacked or abbreviated version, and "B&Co" is not a thing.

Beets & Co

Do

  • Keep the ampersand in Instrument Serif italic and one step quieter than the words either side — stone on soil, beet-deep on paper.
  • Set the words in Bricolage Grotesque 600 at letter-spacing:-.03em. The tightness is what stops it reading as a default sans.
  • Sit the mark on flat colour, or on the darkest, calmest part of a photograph under the veil.
  • Sentence case. Beets & Co — never all caps, never lowercase.

Don't

  • Set the ampersand in Bricolage. It becomes an ordinary business name the moment you do.
  • Swap in "and", "+" or "n". The character is the mark.
  • Add a container, stroke, shadow or gradient. Everything here is flat.
  • Use the beetroot as a logo. It is a concept for the site's imagery, not a mark — see section 05.
02

A dark ground, and one thing that isn't

Sourcebeets-v2.css · :root

Most agencies serving trades go high-contrast safety colours — hi-vis, steel, black and yellow. This palette deliberately doesn't: soil, paper, stone and a beetroot pink that no builder's marketing looks like. It is a studio palette shown to trade clients, which is the whole positioning in five hex values.

Core

Soil #0B0D0A The default ground for the site, decks and social. Green-biased near-black, not neutral — it sits under beet without going purple, and under photography without going flat. ≈ 60% — ground
Soil Lift #1C211A The band that separates a section without drawing a line — journal cards, CTA bands, alternating strata. One step up, never two. Structural
Paper #F2EFE6 All type on soil, and the inverted ground for the Studio and Process strata. Warm off-white — never pure white, which reads cold against this green-black. ≈ 25% — type
Stone #BFB6A2 Body copy, secondary paragraphs, the ampersand, nav links at rest. The quiet voice — 9.69:1 on soil, so it is fully readable, not a whisper. Text tint
Beet #C96C8C The signature. Hover states, accent words, focus rings, links in articles, the open accordion. On dark only — on paper it drops to Beet Deep. ≈ 8% — accent
Clay #C24B33 Action, and only action. Every button on the site is clay and nothing else is. It is a fill colour, not a type colour — see the two derived tints below. ≈ 4% — CTAs

Derived — the three tints the core set can't supply

Beet and clay are both mid-tone, which means neither can legally carry small type on both grounds. Rather than add a colour, deepen or lift the two that exist. Beet Deep is already in the stylesheet; the two clays are the addition.

Beet Deep #6E1B36 Beet on a paper ground — 9.75:1, where flat beet manages 3.04 and fails for anything under 24px. Already wired as .paper .beet. Also the only permitted flat colour ground other than soil. Type on paper · fills
Clay Lift #D45A41 Clay as type on soil. Flat clay measures 4.04:1 there and fails body size; this clears 4.96. Already the button hover value, so the accessible tint was in the file before it was named. Type on soil
Clay Deep #B54129 The button ground, and clay as type on paper. Near-white on flat clay measures 4.34:1 — under AA for a 16px button label. On this it clears 5.02, and the colour shift is imperceptible next to the old one. Button grounds

Three one-line fixes for beets-v2.css

1 · --stone-2 is too faint. At rgba(191,182,162,.62) it resolves to #7B7668 — 4.31:1 on soil, and only 3.61 on the soil-lift band where journal cards and the CTA sit. It carries figcaptions, footer links, form microcopy and post metadata at 16px, so it needs AA. Raise it to .72: 5.45 on soil, 4.58 on the lift band, and visually almost identical.

2 · The button label is under AA. #F7F2EB on --clay is 4.34:1 — fine for the 18px submit button, short for the 16px nav button. Change the button ground to --clay-deep:#B54129 and keep #D45A41 as the hover. Clay stays exactly as it is everywhere else.

3 · The paper-stratum numerals disappear. rgba(20,23,15,.45) on the process steps and .5 on the .when line measure 2.87 and 3.33. Take both to .60 (4.54). They are the only failing values on the light strata — .copy at .68 already clears 5.88.

04

Type rules

  • The tracking is the brand. Bricolage at default spacing looks like any other geometric sans. Every display size sets negative tracking, and the bigger it gets, the tighter it goes.
  • Headlines break to three lines maximum and use text-wrap:balance. A four-line hero means the sentence is too long, not that the type is too big.
  • One serif word per surface. It goes on the noun that matters — rebuild, brand, room — never on a verb and never on two words at once.
  • No uppercase headlines anywhere. Small uppercase is allowed for eyebrows and metadata at 13–14px with letter-spacing:.06em.
  • Never letterspace body copy, never justify it, and never centre a paragraph.

Licensing & substitution

  • All three faces are SIL Open Font Licence via Google Fonts — free to install on any machine, embed in any PDF, and hand to any supplier. There is no licence conversation to have.
  • Web stack: "Bricolage Grotesque","Hanken Grotesk",system-ui,sans-serif for display, "Hanken Grotesk",system-ui,-apple-system,sans-serif for body.
  • Bricolage is a variable font with an optical-size axis (opsz 12–96). Let it track the actual size; don't pin it.
  • Word, Google Docs and Keynote: install all three rather than substituting. A proposal set in Calibri undoes the point of the brand at the exact moment it's being judged.
  • Never Inter, Space Grotesk or General Sans as a stand-in. They are close enough to read as a mistake rather than a decision.
05

Four devices already running on the site

Sourcebeets-v2.css · components

None of these are invented for this document — each is already in the stylesheet. Naming them makes them repeatable, so a story cover made on a phone and a proposal cover made in InDesign come out of the same system.

Better jobs

The veil

Three-stop gradient over every photograph: .62 at the top, .42 at 38%, .94 at the foot. Type always lands on the dark end. The brand never sets text over a bare photo.

Website designLocal SEO

The hairline row

Numbered rows separated by 1px rgba(242,239,230,.15), that indent 16–18px and turn beet on hover. It is the site's main structural grammar — services, process, FAQ, related links all use it.

&

The serif note

Instrument Serif italic, once per surface, at the same size as its neighbours. The ampersand in the mark, one word in a headline, a pull-quote. It is warmth in an otherwise cold system.

ActionStart a conversation

The clay pill — 2px

border-radius:2px on buttons, 3px on images. Nothing else in the brand is rounded. It reads as square from a metre away and as considered up close, which is the point.

Photography direction

  • Shot on the client's own site, in one day, by Michael. Library imagery is not an option — the entire pitch is "I come to you", and stock contradicts it in one frame.
  • Warm, low-contrast grade with the blacks lifted slightly. The veil does the darkening; the file underneath should not already be crushed.
  • Wide enough to show context — the street, the neighbouring build, the crew. Context is what proves the job was real and local.
  • Shoot the person as well as the work. For a one-person studio selling access to a senior partner, a face is a credential.
  • Portrait 4:5 for anything heading to the feed; 16:8 for the site's film strip.

Never

  • Rounded cards, soft shadows, glassmorphism, or a gradient used as decoration rather than as a veil over an image.
  • Hi-vis yellow, safety orange, or any blue. The palette's whole argument is that it doesn't look like trade marketing.
  • Icons in circles, three-up feature grids with a coloured icon at the top — the layout every other agency site uses.
  • Dollar figures on any public surface. Projects are scoped and quoted; the site says so and the social should too.
  • Stock photography of laptops, handshakes, or a hard hat on a set of drawings.

The nine doors

The site's one genuinely proprietary device: nine places a customer can find a business, set as a wrapping row of display type with the live ones in paper and the dead ones in stone. It is the Walk-In Check in a single graphic, and it works on a slide, a story and a page without being redrawn.

06

First person, short sentences, no adjectives doing work

Sourceindex.html · live copy

The site speaks as Michael, not as a company — "I take a handful of clients", not "we deliver tailored solutions". That choice is load-bearing: the product being sold is direct access to one senior person, so a corporate "we" would contradict the offer in the first sentence.

01

Lead with the thing that's true

"Better jobs don't find you." Not a benefit, not a promise — an observation the reader already agrees with before they've read the second line.

02

Name the cost, not just the win

"It takes about eight weeks." "Two client spots open this quarter." Constraints read as honesty and do more selling than superlatives.

03

Specific over vague, always

Volvo, ADF, Nando's. Chelsea, Bonbeach, Mordialloc. Six weeks. Named in ChatGPT and Gemini. Real nouns beat every adjective available.

04

Short sentences. Then a shorter one.

Most paragraphs run two or three sentences. Anything over four lines on a phone gets cut, not condensed.

05

Never quote a price

Public surfaces say scoped, fixed-price and individually quoted — never a figure. Numbers are for the proposal, where there's context around them.

The same sentence, twice

Off-brand We leverage holistic brand solutions to deliver world-class digital experiences for our clients. Corporate we, four buzzwords, nothing a builder can picture.
On-brand I build the brand, the site and the film. Same person, start to finish. First person, concrete nouns, the offer stated as a fact.
Off-brand Unlock your business's full potential with our cutting-edge SEO strategies! Exclamation mark, unfalsifiable claim, no suburb, no mechanism.
On-brand Local SEO across Bayside and South East Melbourne — map pack, listings, reviews and suburb pages. The unglamorous layer that compounds. Names the work, names the places, and admits it isn't exciting.

Words that don't appear

leveragesynergyholisticsolutions world-classcutting-edgeunlockelevate bespokepassionategame-changingseamless

Also out: exclamation marks, rhetorical questions as headlines, and "we" in any sentence describing who does the work. We is only correct when it means Michael and the client together — "what we're building".

07

Six pillars, three posts a week

SourceExtended · with the Post Studio

An agency's grid has a harder job than a client's: it has to be the portfolio and the proof that the studio can do this for someone else. Three feed posts a week — Monday teach, Wednesday show, Friday prove. Every post belongs to one pillar, and each pillar answers a specific doubt a trade owner has about hiring a marketing person at all.

Pillar 1

The Work

A finished brand, site or film — the client's name on it."Can you actually do this?"

Pillar 2

The Number

One measured outcome. Rankings, enquiries, weeks to launch."Does any of it work?"

Pillar 3

The Proof

A client in their own words, verbatim, named."Would someone like me hire him?"

Pillar 4

The Nine Doors

Carousels teaching one door at a time, tied to the Walk-In Check.Answers what they're already Googling.

Pillar 5

Michael

On site, on set, talking. The face behind "you get me"."Who am I actually dealing with?"

Pillar 6

The Position

A flat statement tile. Availability, a point of view, a line from the site.Makes the grid read as a studio, not a feed.

Grid rhythm

Post in threes so the grid always resolves at 4:5. Two image tiles, then one flat brand tile — a statement, a number or a knowledge card. The flat tile is what stops the profile reading as a camera roll. Never two flat tiles in a row, and never a full row of them.

Non-negotiables

  • Flat tiles alternate ground: soil, then paper, then beet-deep. Three dark tiles in a row and the grid turns to mud.
  • Beet is a rule, a plate edge or one word — never a whole tile background behind body copy.
  • Client work is posted with the client named, or not at all. An unattributed mockup proves nothing.
  • Reviews quoted verbatim with a first name and source. Never tidied, never composited from two reviews.
  • No prices, no "packages from", no discount language. Ever.
  • Max three lines of Bricolage on any tile. If it needs four, it's a carousel.

Caption voice

Write the way Michael would explain it standing in someone's yard. Lead with what happened, name the client and the suburb, give one useful detail, end with the one action. No hype, no emoji strings, no "🔥 DROP".

Crowncon had genuinely good work and no way for anyone to find it. Six weeks: new brand, new site, listings claimed, and a film shot on one of their own builds. The stills and the film came from the same day on site — that's the only way it's affordable for a builder. They now come up when you ask ChatGPT or Gemini to recommend a custom home builder in south east Melbourne. Verified August 2026. Nine places a customer can find you. Free check in the bio.

Still open

  • There is no vector master of the mark — it renders from live type everywhere. Worth outlining a proper SVG before any signwriting, embroidery or vehicle quote.
  • The beetroot growth concept — soil line, seed cluster, thinning — is a strong idea and not on the site. It belongs either in section 05 as a named device or nowhere; a half-used metaphor is worse than none.
  • Proposal and invoice documents haven't adopted the system. They should take the soil ground and the Bricolage header treatment so the paperwork matches the pitch.
  • No dark-on-light variant of the Instagram templates has been tested at thumbnail size on a phone in daylight. Worth one real check before the paper tiles go into rotation.

Identity facts to keep consistent

  • Public name is Beets & Co — with the serif ampersand wherever type allows it.
  • Two email addresses are in circulation: the live site and llms.txt use michael@beetsandco.com.au; the project instructions say hello@. Pick one and change the other — a mismatch across public surfaces costs trust and confuses the crawlers.
  • One phone number everywhere: +61 447 542 930.
  • Service area is Bayside and South East Melbourne, with Greater Melbourne as the second line — never "Australia-wide".
  • Instagram handle @beetsandco. The grid and the site should always be one click apart.
